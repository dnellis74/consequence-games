Where this came from

The first time I played this kind of game was Surround, one of the cartridges that launched with the Atari 2600 in 1977. Surround was a home version of Blockade, a 1976 arcade game from Gremlin: two lines, each leaving a wall behind it, and whoever crashed first lost. Twenty years later the same idea became Snake on Nokia phones.

All of those ran on a grid and turned at right angles, because that is what the hardware and the buttons allowed. Blob was my attempt to update the idea for a touchscreen: no grid, curves instead of corners, and you steer with your finger.

This version turns it around again. Here the trail belongs to your pursuer, a snail. In 2014, on the Rooster Teeth podcast, Gavin Free asked whether you would take $10 million and immortality if an immortal snail followed you forever and its touch killed you. It became a meme, the immortal snail, also called the snail assassin. Snails were chasing people long before that: scribes in the 13th and 14th centuries drew armored knights fighting snails in the margins of manuscripts, and the knights were often losing. One theory, from the scholar Lilian Randall, is that the snails stood for Lombard moneylenders.

So money piles up while you run, everything you pick up makes you slower, and the snail never stops. Its trail never dries, and you can't cross it. The score is the one Blockade used, updated: how long you last, counted in dollars.
