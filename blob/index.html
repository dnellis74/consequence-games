Where this came from

The first time I played this kind of game was Surround, one of the cartridges that launched with the Atari 2600 in 1977. Two lines on a screen, each leaving a wall behind it, and whoever crashed first lost.

Surround was a home version of Blockade, a 1976 arcade game from Gremlin. Twenty years later the same idea showed up on Nokia phones as Snake, and a whole generation played it on a number pad.

All of those games ran on a grid and turned at right angles, because that is what the hardware and the buttons allowed. Blob asks what the idea looks like on a touchscreen. The grid is gone. The blob moves in curves, slows down for sharp turns, and follows your finger. You aren't the line anymore. You're what it's chasing.

The score is the same one Blockade used: how long you last.

David Nellis, consequence.games
